> ✎ This is an example page, and it's yours: fill the blanks, keep the headings or don't. The lines marked ✎ are a tour. Clearpen never writes a reflection for you; it asks, and it checks. Delete it all when you're done.

## How I feel

I feel ______ today, because ______.

> ✎ Finish the sentence and pause. Clearpen reads the paragraph back: when it has made its point, given a reason and said why it matters, you see “¶ point made”. When it hasn't, Clearpen may offer the one sentence that supplies what is missing, in grey, and only that one; Tab keeps it, typing on ignores it. ⋯ → Next-sentence suggestions turns this off.

## What happened

Obviously the meeting went badly, and it is a complex issue with many factors. [What happened, in order, as a camera would have seen it.]

> ✎ Press ⌘⇧E. Review doesn't tell you how to feel. Clearpen's rules note that the first word asserts instead of showing, and that the second half is safe because it cannot be disagreed with: what is the strongest version you know to be true? The AI's notes are a reader's reactions, beside the passage, never a rewrite.

## What I expected

Perhaps I might possibly have expected too much, or maybe not enough. [What you expected, and when you decided that.]

> ✎ Four hedges in one sentence blur what you mean. Select the sentence and choose Check ▾ → Check how it reads: Clearpen says what a reader does at each sentence and never rewrites it. The confident tone under Tone ▾ cuts only the hedges that carry nothing; “I think” stays.

## What I would tell a friend

The only way through this is to say it out loud, and writing it down is the next best thing as well.

> ✎ Review flags a false choice, asking for the alternatives, and a sentence that ends on an afterthought, because a reader stresses whatever comes last. Select the sentence and choose Improve: only the words that change are marked, and Esc keeps your original.

## Questions to sit with

The coach's questions, for when the page is blank. Clearpen asks the same kind, because a question joins your own thinking where an answer would replace it, and it never answers them for you.

- What happened, exactly, before I decided how I felt about it?
- What did I expect, and when did I decide that?
- What would I say to a friend who told me this?
- What is the strongest version of this that I know to be true?
- What would make tomorrow a little different?

> ✎ When you've answered one, press ⌘⇧E again. The review reads the whole page as a reader would, in order of importance: the thought first, then its shape, then the sentences. A paragraph that hasn't made its point gets one note saying what it is missing, and the sentence notes wait under it.
