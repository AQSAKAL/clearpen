Last spring our support queue was a shared password and a lot of goodwill. [Your team] answered [n] requests a month from that queue, and by Friday none of us could say which of them had been answered. This is the story of how we moved to a shared inbox, what it cost, and what it fixed.

## The problem

Every customer hates waiting, and 68% of customers say they leave after one slow reply. Our median first reply took [x] hours, and on a bad Monday it took two days. We were losing people we never heard from again.

## What we did

We did not buy anything at first. First, we wrote down every kind of request we got in a week. Then we picked the three kinds that made up most of the queue and wrote a reply for each, which we call a macro. Next, we gave the queue one owner per day instead of all of us at once. Finally, we switched to [the tool], which we utilise to assign and tag each thread.

## What changed

The move paid for itself in three ways:

- Requests reach whoever is free, so the median reply fell from [x] hours to [y].
- Nothing is lost: each thread has an owner and a state.
- Replies are consistent, because the macros are shared.
- Managers see the load without asking.
